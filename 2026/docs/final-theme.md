# That Night in Bethlehem

**Crib 2026 · 16 × 9 ft stage**
*Tagline for the plaque: "See it twice: once in the light, once in the dark."*

## The idea in one paragraph

The Nativity as it most likely happened: a limestone hillside village in Judea on a winter night. The crowded family house has no space in its upper guest room, so the birth happens in the cave beneath it where the animals are kept. The shepherds are in the terraced fields below, Herod's fortress stands on the horizon, and the Magi's caravan is still far off on the road from the east. In daylight it is a museum-quality, hyper-real diorama that looks a kilometre deep. In the dark it becomes a 4-minute show: lamps flicker on, mist fills the valley, stars come out, an angel appears in mid-air over the shepherds, and an angel choir that nobody could see before appears across the sky. The light then travels down the hill into the cave, where the Child is.

- **Raked ground:** the base rises from 0 at the front to 2 ft at the back, using stepped plywood decks and carved foam. This alone adds a lot of apparent depth, and everyone can see the back.
- **Scale tiers (forced perspective):**

| Depth from front | Scale | Figure height | What's there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2.5 ft | 1:3 | 2 ft | Holy Family, ox, donkey, manger, front olive tree |
| 2.5–4.5 ft | 1:6 | 1–1.2 ft | Shepherds, sheep, census soldier, upper-room travellers |
| 4.5–6.5 ft | 1:12 | 5–6 in | Terraces, sheepfold, street houses |
| 6.5–8 ft | 1:24 | 2.5–3 in | Village on the ridge, watchtower, Magi caravan |
| backdrop | painted | — | Hills, Herodium, Jerusalem lights, sky |

**Rule:** objects get smaller, paler and bluer the further back they are (atmospheric perspective). Paint the back houses 30% lighter and cooler than the front ones.

## 3. The heart: the house and the cave

- **Upper floor (guest room, the "kataluma"):** a stone-walled room 4 × 2 ft, cut open at the front like a doll's house. It is crowded with 1:6 travellers asleep on mats, bundles, clay jars and small flickering oil lamps. This shows "no room" exactly, without inventing an inn.
- **Lower level (the cave):** a rough limestone cave arch 5 ft wide × 2.5 ft high × 2.5 ft deep, carved into the hill under the house. It holds a **stone manger** (a carved trough, not a wooden crib), straw, the ox and the donkey, Mary seated and Joseph standing with a clay lamp. The Child lies in the manger.
- **Stair:** rough steps cut in the rock connect the street to the cave mouth, with one shepherd already arriving.

## 6. The innovations explained

1. **Pepper's-ghost floating angel:** a clear 3 mm acrylic sheet (3 × 2 ft) stands at 45° behind a rock ridge, facing the audience. A tablet or monitor lies flat below it, hidden, playing a short video of a glowing angel on a black background. Viewers see the angel hovering over the field with real objects behind it. Test the angle from the viewing spot.
2. **UV-reveal choir:** paint angels on the dark-blue backdrop with invisible or clear UV-reactive paint (or with white fluorescent paint thinned on matte blue). In normal light they are invisible; under UV they glow. This is the moment people will remember.
3. **Fibre-optic stars:** push 200–300 fibre ends through the backdrop; a single illuminator with a twinkle wheel makes them shimmer. It looks far more real than LED strings.
4. **Shadow of the cross:** the only "message" moment, done with one light and two real objects, with no words.
5. **Forced perspective, raked stage and atmospheric colour:** the daylight wow, with no electricity needed.
6. **Real mist and water:** the ultrasonic mister lies in a hidden water tray in the valley; the mist settles low and catches the light during the angel moment.
7. **Path of light:** addressable LED strip (WS2812) buried under sand on the path; it chases toward the cave on cue.
8. **Sound and scent:** sheep bells, crickets, a single voice for the angel line, a soft choir at the reveal; frankincense at the front.
